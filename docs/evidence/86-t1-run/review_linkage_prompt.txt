Project "Raptor": ostrich/raptor-like biped robot, goal 40 km/h (11.1 m/s) with tail balance, then turning, disturbance recovery, standing, exploration. Graduation project, Mac M5 CPU, MuJoCo + PPO. Tiers: T1 virtual actuator sim, T2 boom (lateral/yaw constrained) + external power, T3 free hardware.

FACTS (measured in T1 unless stated):
- Current best policy v8d (12 active axes: hip roll/pitch, knee, ankle pitch/roll per leg + tail yaw/pitch; parallel springs, clutched ankle spring): 9.08 m/s at the 11.1 m/s command without falls, 2.9 Hz, 3.2 BW, CoT 0.6. Weak: cannot stand still, weak turning, yaw impulse tolerance 1.0 N·m·s.
- Horse-style 1:1 knee-ankle coupling (ankle pitch motors removed, 10 active axes): 8.28 m/s at the 11.1 command without falls, tolerates 1.5 N·m·s yaw impulses, CoT +30 %, relies more on the tail.
- Whole-robot actuator budget with commercial QDD classes (A 0.5 kg 22 N·m 40 rad/s, B 0.96 kg 48 N·m 33 rad/s, C 1.42 kg 120 N·m 21 rad/s), heat check RMS <= 40 % of peak, feasibility = actuators <= 65 % of total mass: no configuration closes at 11.1 m/s (knee needs 49 N·m at 21 rad/s and RMS beyond B continuous; C too slow); hardware closes only ~4-6 m/s at ~11 kg with knee-ankle coupling and fewer axes. Loads per leg at 11.36 kg, springs (c): hip 60 N·m / 20.6 rad/s / 614 W peak, knee 49 N·m / 21.1 rad/s, ankle 13 N·m.
- Mass layout: CoM 0.066 m behind the hip; an ostrich stance (femur 65 deg forward, intertarsal 155 deg) needs the hip moved ~0.2-0.3 m back relative to the torso payload (R-03 candidate); tail kept.
- KAIST Raptor (IEEE Spectrum): one motor per leg driving a 9-bar linkage with an Achilles-like tendon spring, ~46 km/h on a treadmill (support conditions not verified by us).
- The user showed a video of a crank-driven closed-loop leg: 4-bar (crank O-D, rocker C-E) + near-parallelogram extension to the foot, 1 DOF per leg, spring element, motor and gears at the hip, long light links. The user explicitly does NOT want "one motor only"; the question is which ELEMENTS benefit the Raptor product.
